The Weakness Trap

"What are you good at?"

Most people can answer what they're bad at in 10 seconds. But ask what they're truly good at — and silence follows.

Weakness? Instant answer.
Strength? Silence.
The Pattern

We're trained to fix what's broken.

In school, in feedback reviews, in annual goals — the message is always the same: Find your deficits. Work harder. Become "well-rounded."

We know our weaknesses in detail — but our strengths remain blurry.

The Real Cost

Where does excellence come from?

Fixing Weaknesses
3→5
Become okay at many things
vs
Building Strengths
8→10
Become exceptional at what matters
Milo had tried to be good at everything.
Instead, he became great at nothing.
The Strengths Shift

Positive Psychology asks a different question.

Instead of "What is wrong with you?" — it asks:

What is right about you — and how do we use that more?

Milo stood at a crossroads. One path felt familiar. The other looked lighter.

The Insight

Strengths aren't "nice to have."

What comes naturally to you? What gives you energy instead of draining it? Where do you create impact without forcing it?

This isn't soft thinking. It's practical — and powerful.
